Assigned 2026-10-03. The user authorized recording all useful directions and
implementing the high-value work as far as evidence and practical dependencies
allow. This supersedes the pending-assignment language in earlier investigations.
It does not change the operational contracts, execute purchases, or authorize

separate offline boundary; the operational demo stays synthetic.

## Execution rules

Finish a small reviewable work package, save exact reproducible results, update
this ledger and relevant docs, then proceed to the next ready package. A block
must record its cause, evidence, unblock condition and the next independent task;
do not stall the whole roadmap. Preserve consumed evaluations as regressions.
Freeze new protocols, traces, candidate grids and gates before observing scores.
After viewing outcomes, that evaluation is consumed: improvements require fresh
later evidence. Reuse valid caches and do not rerun old studies for presentation.

Distinguish implemented, validated, published, measured, proposed and blocked.
No automatic operational promotion, population confidence, commercial savings,
or service guarantee follows from a small synthetic experiment. Public sales
do not establish unconstrained demand or historical inventory availability.

## Wave 1: fresh decisions and calibrated uncertainty

1. **Fresh demand/supplier traces and costed warmup — implemented and validated.**
   [Frozen protocol](RESEARCH_WARMUP_V1.md). Use new demand seed namespaces,
   independently seeded supplier paths per origin block, paired cold/warm arms,
   policy-owned carryover, later origin blocks, common settlement dates and exact
   acquisition/holding/backlog/setup accounting. Preserve unsuccessful and null
   controls. Acceptance: independent state, cost, timing, conservation and
   no-future-information oracles; reproducible inputs and source hashes.
   [Measured result](FRESH_WARMUP_RESULTS.md): 360 pairs/720 arms, 18 distinct
   generated demand paths and 72 supplier paths; 44 focused tests pass. Warmup
   improves fill in 70 pairs, regresses it in 35, and lowers full cost in two.
2. **Service feasibility attribution — implemented as bounded interventions.** Preserve the independent
   earliest-receipt bound. Compare initial stock, lead, supplier delay, review
   calendar and packs/MOQ using predeclared interventions. Report signed paired
   effects and interaction/residual, rather than inventing an additive causal
   partition. Initial feasibility and subsequent policy misses remain distinct.
   [Supply/intervention results](SUPPLY_SENSITIVITY_RESULTS.md) retain startup
   bounds and signed stock/review/pack/lead/supplier effects; no fabricated
   additive causal shortage partition.
3. **Nominal safety versus achieved service — initial study measured.** Compare
   fixed zero safety and empirical 90%/95% targets with actual fill and complete
   cycle service, cumulative target coverage and pinball loss. Report all sample
   counts; adjacent quantiles may select the same empirical rank. Targets are
   forecast uncertainty inputs, never asserted service guarantees.
4. **Safety retention under demand decline — implemented and measured.** Freeze
   expanding, recent-window and one decay-weighted residual policy; keep forecast
   identical. Test temporary pause/recovery, seasonal lows, gradual decline and
   permanent cessation. Acceptance: completed calibration labels only, minimum
   samples, current safety, owned stock, recovery-period service and full cost.
   No automatic retirement or free stock liquidation.
   [Fresh retention result](SAFETY_RETENTION_RESULTS.md): 105 simulations,
   17 distinct demand paths, recent0/21 and decay0/21 descriptive dual-reference
   passes. Final safety shrinks to zero on permanent cessation, but owned stock
   remains and pause-recovery service can regress. Eight new oracles pass.
5. **Lead-time and supplier-reliability study — implemented and measured.** Lead 2/5/10 days,
   low/medium/high hidden variability; use absolute calendar supplier traces and
   a common settlement end. Hold forecast/policy fixed while changing supply,
   and supply fixed while changing forecast. Report family/path results and
   interactions; cost repricings are not independent replications.
   351 pairs/702 logical arms over seven distinct demand paths; expected supplier
   delay held at one day while variability changes. All settlement/cost checks
   pass, and effects remain path-dependent. Five new oracles pass.
6. **Forecast uncertainty — starts with target coverage/pinball in Wave 1.**
   Extend to cumulative protection-period quantiles and empirical coverage on
   fresh labels if the initial gaps warrant it. No new dependency required.
7. **Small policy comparison — ready after timing adapter protocol.** Compare
   order-up-to, reorder-point and current prefix protection with the same
   forecast, stock, reservations and supply. Define review calendar and action
   timing before comparison. Three defensible policies are sufficient.

## Wave 2: public observed-sales realism

8. **Public-data adapter and provenance — assigned, ready.** Implement synthetic
   row oracles before acquiring official UCI Online Retail. Recheck official
   source and CC BY 4.0; retain archive/member SHA-256, acquisition time, schema,
   raw row identities, cancellations, invalid rows, duplicate diagnostics and
   deterministic item/retailer mapping. Raw rows and reconstructable series stay
   local and ignored. No country-as-warehouse mapping or invented source clock.
9. **Public observed-sales benchmark — assigned, follows adapter acceptance.**
   Follow [public-sales protocol](PUBLIC_SALES_PROTOCOL_V1.md), train-only item
   sampling and frozen train/selection/holdout boundaries. Report sparsity,
   daily/cumulative error and exact segment denominators. Synthetic data remains
   the authority for stockout semantics and correctness; public observations
   provide external realism. Any inventory experiment is a sales-proxy scenario.
10. **Advanced-model gate — conditional.** Test ADIDA/IMAPA only after repeated
    held-out cumulative baseline weakness across diverse observed-sales series.
    LightGBM/global models need a frozen feature/release/selection protocol and
    an independently measured gain worth the dependency. If baselines hold up,
    record a negative gate decision and stop the model expansion.

## Wave 3: system evidence and domain depth

11. **Stronger provenance — starts with Wave 1 manifests.** Source/transform/
    forecast/plan/simulation/UI lineage, code/config hashes, immutable dependency
    manifest and deterministic replay. Add only the missing evidence links.
12. **Cross-layer property/mutation QA — ready alongside affected packages.**
    Prioritize conservation, knowledge-clock leakage, missing versus zero,
    identity collisions, backend/UI JSON equality and replay determinism. Use a
    small purposeful mutation set that proves guards detect a fault; avoid
    test-count-driven expansion or unrelated full-suite reruns.
13. **Backlog versus lost sales — separately versioned protocol first.** Preserve
    accepted-order backlog semantics; create a research-only lost-sales kernel,
    opportunity penalty and service denominator. Compare behavior and costs;
    never silently reinterpret existing orders or historical artifacts.
14. **Planner execution adapter / closed loop — protocol and feasibility first.**
    Define recommendation-to-action identity, simulated receipts and repeated
    origin-known state. Execute only in synthetic simulation. Requires the
    policy comparison boundary; this is larger than advisory presentation.
15. **Physical count truth layer — additive synthetic protocol first.** Separate
    ledger consistency from physical truth. Preserve count timestamp, observer,
    confidence, recount and adjustment evidence; uncertainty cannot become a
    pass or an automatic source repair. Stage 1 contract changes need review.
16. **Performance/scale benchmark — optional, evidence-triggered.** Predeclare
    10k SKU and 100k/1M movement workloads only if latency or job target warrants
    it. Record environment, sizes, DB plans and query/batch/API timing; no
    production-scale claim from toy results or unnecessary large regeneration.
17. **Multi-location allocation — deferred scope gate.** First define transfer
    availability, transit and opportunity costs. Add only after single-location
    evidence identifies a decision that transfers could improve.
18. **Supplier capacity/calendar constraints — deferred scope gate.** Existing
    MOQ/pack constraints remain. Capacity, blackout dates and order calendars
    require explicit evidence and frozen semantics; avoid speculative ERP scope.
